## Question

Is Arlington’s resident-access structure for electronic Town Meeting advocacy constitutionally vulnerable, and what is the narrowest remedy?

### Dispute

Arlington’s post-COVID Town Meeting communication system gave elected officials and insiders reliable access to a Town-controlled channel while forcing dissenting residents into sponsor dependence, subjective Moderator review, private-email BCC workarounds, and mass blocking. The problem is not that officials disagreed with Harrington. The problem is that the Town maintained a communication architecture that predictably filters disfavored resident advocacy before it reaches the legislative body.

After Arlington Town Meeting moved into electronic processes, resident access to Town Meeting became dependent on insider permission, Moderator discretion, and unstable private-email workarounds.

### Remedy

The Town needs a neutral, durable, resident-access channel to Town Meeting. Arlington should establish a resident-to-Town-Meeting electronic submission channel. Any resident may submit written advocacy concerning a pending warrant article. Submissions should be archived publicly and distributed to TMMs by digest or link. The Moderator may impose neutral limits on timing, format, length, malware, privacy, true threats, and non-germane commercial content, but may not reject submissions based on viewpoint, tone, criticism of officials, or subjective civility/appropriateness. Any rejection must be written, cite the rule relied on, and be appealable or logged publicly.

## Conclusion

In 2022, the Town moved into an electronic format that structurally impaired ordinary resident petitioning; resident objections to that impairment were not given equal access to Town Meeting; and when Town Meeting itself voted on whether to proceed electronically, the Moderator barred merits debate on the format that created the access problem.

Article 14 shows resident advocacy directly relevant to a pending warrant article was denied access to the Town Meeting channel because the speaker was a resident rather than an insider.

If the TMM Email List is a Town service, it is government infrastructure. Even if the Town calls it “not a public forum,” its access rules must still be reasonable, clear, and viewpoint-neutral. A system that gives insiders reliable access while residents must rely on sponsorship, Moderator discretion, or broken workarounds is difficult to defend as civic equality.

The issue is not manners. The issue is whether vague civility and appropriateness concepts are being used as enforceable speech filters in a Town-controlled communication system.

Harrington demanded an immediate end to content-based restrictions and implementation of an unblockable, Town-administered communication channel — such as a resident remailer — to restore lawful public access and transparency to the Town Meeting process.  There was no reply nor was the demand added to the TM public record.

#### Remedy

Any Arlington resident may submit written advocacy concerning a pending warrant article. Submissions shall be archived publicly and distributed to Town Meeting Members by digest, link, or resident remailer. The Town may impose neutral limits on timing, length, format, malware, privacy, and true threats. The Town may not reject submissions based on viewpoint, criticism of officials, tone, subjective civility, or undefined “appropriateness.” Any rejection must be written, cite the rule relied upon, and be preserved in a public log.
